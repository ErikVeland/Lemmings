# 2.1 roadmap

Updated 20 October 2026. Prioritised delivery plan following the preservation and newcomer review.
Implementation, inspected renders, human playtests and device acceptance are tracked separately.

## Release purpose

Make the original puzzles comfortable to discover, play and revisit across
generations. Preserve the humour, pixel artwork, rules and immediate controls.
Complete access, teaching, campaign and reliability work before expanding optional
emotional presentation. Do not add content merely to increase the catalogue.

The owner authorised this order on 19 October 2026. Basic magnification is a
standard control. Original presentation and modern conveniences are independent.
Missing level-specific help must be unmistakable before general coaching appears.

### First half-hour

Use the existing Journey and original introductory levels to teach through play.
Do not require a technology/effects choice before the first successful assignment.
Keep Pause, Retry, Rewind where supported, Hints and free Try later discoverable.
Do not add mandatory explanations, tutorial overlays or repeat instructions that
already appear in the controls. Preserve quick access for experienced players.
Include existing welcome, privacy and optional music-download steps in the first
session observations. Defer optional setup when it delays the first assignment.

The 227-lesson curriculum remains Classic-only. Do not rebalance every puzzle or
add compulsory beginner lessons based on replay workload estimates. Observe a
small varied group first, including children, non-gamers, returning players and
veterans. Record confusion, recovery, voluntary retry and next-day resume as well
as completion. No human sessions have been completed by this roadmap update.

### Hints that teach a plan

Keep exact level/engine matching and deliberate spoiler tiers. Human-author
obstacle-specific reasoning for observed walls, with a checked route as evidence.
Explain the worker/crowd relationship, safe landing, containment or tool budget
before revealing assignments. Do not equate a recorded opening skill with the
puzzle's only insight or easiest solution. General mechanics coaching stays useful
but explicitly separate. L2/L3 need exact native adapters and route-backed hints
before any claim of equivalent level-specific coverage.

### Device and language acceptance

Preserve scalable bitmap text, reduced motion and reduced flashes. Distinguish
eligible and ineligible assignment targets by shape as well as colour without
changing hit geometry. Verify pointer, touch, keyboard, controller and remote
paths in every supported native engine. Human-review instructions and important
failure/recovery text in each translated language. English fallback remains
preferable to inventing a reviewed translation.

Keep automated work offscreen and muted. Physical input, real VoiceOver journeys,
listening, HDR appearance, sustained thermal performance and live cloud propagation
remain separate recorded checks. Do not change displays, take desktop focus or
run audible tests without the explicit request required by AGENTS.md.

## Rules for the release

- Add no required screen, minimum viewing time, automatic pause, camera move or
  extra confirmation to starting, retrying or continuing a level.
- Keep the current primary actions and shortcuts. A successful clear continues;
  a failed attempt retries. Optional rescue goals do not block either action.
- Preserve all physics, timing, skill eligibility, rescue requirements, native
  medals, carry-over rules and leaderboard eligibility.
- Keep live sprites, targeting and skill feedback clear. No floating names,
  speech bubbles, death notices or narrative overlays during active play.
- Use the shipped bitmap lettering, sprites and shared stone controls. Follow
  [UI principles](UIPrinciples.md), `GameControls.swift`, `GameStoneButton` and
  the bitmap renderers. Add detail to existing review flows before adding pages.
- Honour mute, reduced motion and reduced flash. New details remain usable
  without sound, hover or colour discrimination.
- Keep every Hot Seat handover paused until Ready. Reviewing a run cannot
  advance the turn, change its owner or resume the simulation.

## Workstream 1 Results and emotional restraint

Priority: required. Start with the shared result model and presentation, then
check the Classic, L2 and L3 hosts on Mac and the shared mobile/TV frontend.

Keep the outcome, saved count, goals and primary action as the main reading
order. Move philosophical interpretation into the existing optional details
flow instead of presenting an unsolicited judgement beside every result.
Keep previously earned titles and records intact. Do not infer the player's
intent or describe a low rescue count as a character flaw.

Retain the final gameplay image behind the existing result layout where the
host can do so without obscuring counters or actions. Use an already available
frame; do not delay results to capture one. A bridge, a remaining blocker or
an empty exit can carry the subtext without a caption. Do not extend simulation
after completion to stage an ending or misreport an alive actor as dead.

Audit the existing near-miss and award copy. Keep useful quantities and action
names. Remove redundant commentary and guilt prompts. Ordinary failure must
still make experimentation feel safe.

Acceptance: compare success, failure, partial rescue, full rescue, unknown
maximum, new record and no-record results in all three games. Controls accept
the first input during every animation. Opening and closing details restores
the same result, focus and ownership. Historical awards remain unchanged.

## Workstream 2 Individual names and observed histories

Priority: required as an optional feature, after the identity feasibility gate.
Default presentation stays anonymous. A player can open inspection explicitly
while paused, without changing settings or answering a first-run prompt. Extend
the same details into run review when its retained observations are available.
See [individual inspection](IndividualLemmings.md) for the first implementation
and its validation limits.

### Names

Use a small, authored, localised name catalogue with restrained names such as
Pip, Moss or Nell. Names have no rarity, bonus, gameplay effect or special death
cue. Do not use the player's contacts, online generation or real personal data.
Avoid joke surnames, famous tragic figures and names that imply a heroic role.

Create a shared presentation identity outside simulation state. Scope it to an
exact level configuration and starting cohort, then map the engine's stable
actor ID or verified spawn ordinal to it. Use a stable, versioned mapping rather
than process-random hashing. Store the catalogue version and identity mapping
with retained observations so an update does not rename a remembered attempt.
Keep names unique within the finite cohort where practical; a spawn number in
details can disambiguate repeated names without crowding the playfield.

Retries with the same starting state reuse names, including across players in
the same Hot Seat. Rewind restores the same identity. Restored checkpoints keep
the mapping. A different starting population or configuration uses its own scope.
Names never establish that an actor is the same person in another level.

### Inspection and histories

Offer inspection only while already paused or in an explicit review context.
On Mac, paused hover reveals the individual immediately in a panel that stays
outside the pointer and receives no input. Keep explicit browsing for longer
histories and non-pointer access.
Its input must be separate from skill assignment. Pointer, touch, keyboard,
controller and accessibility users need an equivalent way to select an actor.
Back returns to the prior paused state. Do not add a persistent live HUD button.

Show a name and at most two short observations, generated from recorded events:

- `Pip` — `Built twice. Reached the exit.`
- `Nell` — `First out. Reached the exit.`
- `Moss` — `Still in the level when the attempt ended.`

These examples specify tone, not existing event coverage. Only show a statement
when that engine supplies the necessary evidence. Never turn “built a bridge”
into “saved twelve friends”, “stood as a blocker” into “chose to sacrifice”, or
“missing from the final frame” into “died”. Record unreleased actors and L3
reserves separately from losses.

Observed history follows the current simulation branch. Rewinding past a loss
removes it from that branch's summary. Do not accumulate deaths, guilt or rescue
credit across rewinds. The existing attempt-level rewind record stays truthful.

Keep event collection bounded and outside the physics loop's critical work.
An observation save failure must preserve the existing run and records. Old
attempts without observations remain reviewable with their available data.

### Optional character details

The intended model is inspecting an individual guest in a theme park or sim.
The player can discover a small life behind a familiar sprite, then return to
the puzzle. Character detail belongs within the optional inspection feature
alongside names and observed histories. It never requests attention on its own.

Give each individual a modest authored preference, want or personal detail.
For example, Pip collects smooth stones and wants one for the collection. Nell
likes company and would welcome a quiet place to sit. A brief detail view can
show one such line beside the name and observed actions. Further history is
available by choice rather than expanding every inspection into a biography.

Treat wants and needs as fictional characterisation. They create no hunger,
happiness or safety meters, obligations, rewards, alerts or extra objectives.
Do not present an invented emotional state as something the engine measured.
Explain the distinction once in feature help; do not repeat a disclaimer on
every character. Keep authored personality separate from recorded history.

Keep personality stable with the name across retries, rewind and restoration.
It cannot change walking, skill acceptance, timing or survival. A lemming who
likes company still follows the same rules as everyone else. Avoid traits that
promise different physical abilities or decisions, such as a fear of heights
that implies refusing to walk off a ledge.

Use restrained, varied writing with room for ordinary humour. Exclude dying
wishes, funerals, moral advice and procedural tragic stories. Do not attach a
personal detail to a death notification or ask the player to save a named
favourite. The player chooses whom to care about and how much to discover.
Long authored biographies and cross-level personal continuity remain outside
2.1. Individual lines must pass human tone review before inclusion.

Acceptance: verify mapping through spawn, multiple entrances, retry, restart,
rewind, checkpoint recovery and Hot Seat ownership in each game. Compare enabled
and disabled runs for identical simulation outputs and compatible records.
No inspection input can assign a skill or unpause the game. Missing event data
omits a claim. Old records and movies gain no invented history.

## Workstream 3 Revisiting attempts

Priority: required. Extend the existing records and replay flows.

Allow a player to keep a run from its existing review flow even when it sets no
record. Retain the available movie and new observation attachment separately
from immutable attempt history. Show unavailable recordings honestly. Cancelling
an export or failing to retain a movie does not block Retry or Continue.

In the existing level records flow, let the player choose two compatible runs
and switch between their reviews. Start with sequential viewing and the saved
count change. Do not build simultaneous playback, an automatic highlight editor
or another live ghost mode for 2.1. Keep manual retention bounded, with explicit
removal of retained media that preserves its statistical attempt record.

Suggest the first clear and strongest compatible rescue only when those movies
exist. Do not promise that the current record-only retention contains every
first clear. Compare assistance conditions explicitly and retain current board
separation. No new reward penalises rewind or rewards repeated losses.

Acceptance: review a failure and later clear, a rescue improvement, assisted and
unassisted attempts, missing media, an old movie without observations, cancelled
retention and a saved Hot Seat. Back restores the originating records or result
page. A movie alone never enables actor selection; inspection requires recorded
actor data linked to the same attempt and time.

## Workstream 4 Music and ending tone

Priority: required tuning of existing effects.

Keep each game's assigned music, the same-composition rescue lift and the current
short star sequence. Preserve the original voices and comic timing. Do not add
music per named lemming or a new dramatic cue per death.

Review failure desaturation, dimming and music recovery together. Restrict any
tuning to the existing failure state; individual losses during a viable run must
not trigger funeral treatment. Retry and rewind recover cleanly through the
existing state machine. Distinguish a musical celebration of meeting the target
from a claim that everyone survived.

Acceptance: muted mechanical checks cover target reached, later loss, nuke and
undo, rewind across the target, immediate retry, result exit, paused handover
and missing alternate music. Audio listening and physical output checks remain
separate, explicitly authorised sessions. Headless checks cannot establish tone.

## Workstream 5 Optional journeys and shared play

Priority: required, using existing playlists and saved-run flows.

Curate one short optional sequence of four to six levels for each native game.
Use a rhythm of discovery, confidence, reversal and relief, with short neutral
titles such as Across or Another Way. Mix playful and demanding moments. Do not
present “grief” as a compulsory chapter or add text between levels.

Select exact level variants with known routes and review their actual play.
Do not label losses inevitable without accepted evidence. Keep authored order,
rescue targets and skill constraints intact. Avoid making a sacrifice solution
the prescribed emotional lesson.

For L2 and L3, review population carry-over and tribe progression before choosing
the sequence. Prefer a contiguous native campaign segment if arbitrary playlist
entries cannot preserve honest starting conditions. Do not fabricate named
survivor transfers to make a mixed sequence seem continuous.

Hot Seat uses its current house rules, silent ghosts and Ready handover. Actor
details identify the attempt owner and whether the selected actor is a ghost.
Never merge different players' observations or attribute a ghost's action to the
live player. Keep shared and solo saved journeys separate.

Acceptance: complete, quit, resume, retry and advance each sequence in solo and
Hot Seat. Check each game's starting population, campaign isolation, ghost
compatibility and owner after return from review. Keep the existing Classic
learning journey and its saved positions intact.

## Validation and human acceptance

Run focused deterministic checks for identity, observation branching, attachment
persistence and unchanged simulation outputs. Run native UI checks through
`Tools/UITestRunner/run.py`, in its default offscreen mode with
`LEMMINGS_TEST_AUDIO=muted`. Keep all automated testing headless and in the
background. Inspect actual renders and input bounds for compact and wide Mac
windows, phone portrait/landscape, tablet and TV layouts where supported.
Keyboard, touch, controller, screen-reader focus and Back must reach the same
details without activating gameplay. Record physical-input and device gaps.

Invite both habitual players and newcomers to try the same short sequences with
the optional layer disabled and enabled, varying the order. Observe immediate
Retry/Continue use, accidental input, whether people voluntarily revisit a run,
and whether the final screen helps them understand the outcome. Ask what they
noticed before prompting them about emotion.

Names pass if people can recognise an individual without searching through
clutter or feeling obliged to read. Character details pass if interested players
can develop affection while others overlook them without disadvantage. Revise
or remove lines that feel repetitive, sentimental, manipulative or at odds with
the slapstick. Check that nobody mistakes a want for a task or a personality
trait for a skill modifier. No target number of sad reactions is a success
criterion. Human review must still find the game funny and enjoyable.

Release evidence must separate automated mechanics, inspected renders, human
responses and unverified device behaviour. This roadmap changes no current
release classification. Update each status only from its recorded implementation
and acceptance evidence; human responses and device behaviour are never inferred.
