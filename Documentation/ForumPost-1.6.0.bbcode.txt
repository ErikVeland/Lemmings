I’ve been building Ultimate Lemmings: an unofficial native macOS project bringing classic Lemmings; Oh No! More Lemmings, the Xmas and Holiday releases, Lemmings 2: The Tribes, and Lemmings 3 into one connected app.

It started with wanting to play Lemmings again. It has grown into something closer to how I remember playing it: sitting with other people, arguing over solutions, taking turns, making spectacular mistakes and cheering when we finally get everyone home.

[ATTACH type="full" size="5118x2880"]2663472[/ATTACH]

[B]How is this different from an emulator or another Lemmings engine?[/B]

An emulator runs the original game executable inside a recreated machine. Ultimate Lemmings reads the original game data and runs its own native engines, built from the ground up to re-create the original simulation with Swift. That lets the games share menus, profiles, controls, QoL, and saved-game flows, while retaining their different gameplay systems.

The aim is to preserve the original puzzles and character while making the whole experience comfortable to play today. That means original campaigns, the sequels, rarely played Macintosh artwork (2x artwork that only appeared in the classic Mac ports, not seen in any other port), shared play and modern controls in one place.

Community engines have already done enormous work keeping Lemmings alive, so of course not claiming that every convenience here is a new invention. What makes this project different is the combination and the emphasis on returning to these games with someone sitting beside you.

[B]Built for taking turns[/B]

Hot Seat and player profiles exist because that is how I played Lemmings as a kid: alternating attempts with my cousins, sisters and friends.

There are separate player profiles, persistent shared saves, clear turn ownership, paused handovers and explicit retry choices. You can leave a shared game, play solo and come back without losing your shared progress.

It gives that old “your turn—let’s see you do better” experience a proper home in the game.

[b]Known limits[/b]

Lemmings 2 has preserved wins for 73 of 120 levels and Lemmings 3 for 41 of 90; both remain preview status. Physical Intel, minimum macOS, HDR, multiple displays and sustained performance are what this candidate exists to test, they are not yet established. Live Game Center services under network loss, full VoiceOver listening journeys and physical controller journeys still need tester validation. Two known bugs remain open from before this candidate: toggling Macintosh-style Lemmings 2 artwork on then off does not always restore the exact original frame, and one controller button-combo sequence (retry, rewind and step together) does not route correctly. Both are unrelated to the changes above.

[B]Why I’m still working on it[/B]

I cannot emphasise enough how well this game has held up.

We are playing it NOW, laughing out loud at how we are fkking up levels and solutions, then cheering when something finally works. The Hot Seat features came directly out of those evenings. It is pure joy to play still and I am sad this IP was left on the vine to die (we speak not of the current gacha mobile games).

For me, this is as much fun in 2026 as it was in 1992. Lemmings is a ridiculously good game, and I want more people to have that experience again.
