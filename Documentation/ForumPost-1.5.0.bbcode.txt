I’ve been building Ultimate Lemmings: an unofficial native macOS project bringing classic Lemmings, Oh No! More Lemmings, the Xmas and Holiday releases, Lemmings 2: The Tribes, and Lemmings 3 into one connected app.

It started with wanting to play Lemmings again. It has grown into something closer to how I remember playing it: sitting with other people, arguing over solutions, taking turns, making spectacular mistakes and cheering when we finally get everyone home.

[b]It’s now at 1.5.0.[/b] There are more than 6,000 fan levels, a proper level browser, playlists, a frankly unreasonable amount of music, and increasingly elaborate ways to recover from the terrible decision you just made. I may have got slightly carried away.

[b]How is this different from an emulator or another Lemmings engine?[/b]

An emulator runs the original game executable inside a recreated machine. Ultimate Lemmings reads the original game data and runs its own native engines. That lets the games share menus, profiles, controls, QoL and saved-game flows, while retaining their different gameplay systems.

The aim is to preserve the original puzzles and character while making the whole experience comfortable to play today. Original campaigns, the sequels, rarely seen Macintosh artwork, shared play and modern controls, all in one place.

Community engines have already done enormous work keeping Lemmings alive, so of course I’m not claiming every convenience here is a new invention. What makes this project different is the combination, and the emphasis on returning to these games with someone sitting beside you.

[b]Built for taking turns[/b]

Hot Seat and player profiles exist because that is how I played Lemmings as a kid: alternating attempts with my cousins, sisters and friends.

There are separate player profiles, persistent shared saves, clear turn ownership, paused handovers and explicit retry choices. You can leave a shared game, play solo and come back without losing your shared progress.

Handovers now accept keyboard actions too, and the game stays paused until the next player is ready. No lemmings marching off a cliff while someone is still getting comfortable.

It gives that old “your turn—let’s see you do better” experience a proper home in the game.

[b]More comfortable to play, without losing the puzzle[/b]

[list]
[*][b]Your choice of controls.[/b] Original, Modern and Custom presets let you choose how much help you want. Mouse, keyboard and controller are supported, with remappable controller bindings and direct skill shortcuts.
[*][b]Less “NO, THE OTHER ONE”.[/b] Approaching-lemming targeting helps with crossing crowds. New options favour a blocker when you’re assigning a bomb, or a builder when you want to extend its bridge. These work across all three games, using Use tool in Lemmings 3. Prefer strict nearest-lemming targeting? Turn the aids off.
[*][b]A cursor that tells you what will happen.[/b] Green corners mean an eligible target, yellow means ineligible, and grey means empty terrain. Choose the size of the skill icon, hide it, or add a nearby lemming count. A subtle halo marks the target and a brief pulse confirms a skill assignment.
[*][b]Fast-forward, rewind and single steps.[/b] Tap to toggle fast-forward, or hold Shift, the Speed button or a controller trigger for a temporary boost. New toolbar buttons rewind two seconds or step one tick backward or forward, leaving play paused. Rewind availability depends on the ruleset.
[*][b]Help over the actual level.[/b] Press H, I or F1, or use the hints button. Left and Right move through the hints, from a gentle nudge towards a solution. The full solution still asks before spoiling it for you.
[*][b]Ready? 3–2–1...[/b] Fresh levels and retries have a cancellable countdown. Pause or single-step to take control of the start. Restored saves stay paused.
[*][b]Saved attempts and replays.[/b] Escape saves your attempt and returns to the menu. Recover interrupted runs, review your wins or export a replay as a video with sound. Saved runs from earlier builds carry forward.
[*][b]Keyboard navigation that reaches the menus too.[/b] Tab, Shift-Tab and arrow keys move through dialogs; Return activates the selected button. Replay and original-movie controls also have keyboard and accessibility actions.
[*][b]Accessibility options.[/b] Scalable menus, reduced motion and flashes, pause on interruption, clearer focus and named controls. The normal mouse pointer now stays visible over menus and controls, with the game reticle over the playfield.
[/list]

[b]Where it stands: 1.5.0 (!)[/b]

The Classic campaign and all 60 Oh Yes! conversions are complete, with 352 verified winning routes. Lemmings 2 and Lemmings 3 are playable previews: L2 has preserved wins for 73 of 120 levels, and L3 for 41 of 90. They still need wider comparison with the original engines.

There is also [b]NeoLemmix preview work[/b], including improved level rendering and a reader for current .nxrp replay files. Full replay and gameplay compatibility is unfinished. Fencer, Laserer, skill pickups and several other mechanics are still unsupported.

Since 1.1 RC2, there have also been plenty of fixes to the less exciting things that matter when you actually sit down to play: disappearing sequel cursors, pause keys toggling twice, speed buttons, clicks behind bridge builders, liquid under bridges, missing hints and rescue targets, and the black window on a fresh installation.

Startup and menus are faster too. With 516 saved runs on my development Mac, finding the newest save went from 6.1 seconds to 0.26 seconds. Returning to a campaign avoids repeated setup, and transition saves no longer block play.

The app checks for signed updates. From 1.5, you get the release notes and choose when to install. This is a universal Mac app for [b]Intel and Apple silicon, running macOS 12.3 or later[/b]. The 1.5 download also covers Monterey. An iPhone or iPad app is not included in this release.

[b]Things I’d love you to try[/b]

Bring an existing save across. Play Hot Seat with someone. Make a playlist. Try the targeting aids in a horrible crowd. Rewind the mistake you were absolutely certain was a brilliant idea. Switch artwork and soundtracks. See how it feels with your controller.

If something breaks, please include your Mac model, macOS version, game and level, and whether you were using a mouse, keyboard or controller. Intel and older-macOS reports are especially useful. Long sessions, sustained fast-forward, HDR and multiple displays still need wider testing, as do complete VoiceOver and physical-controller journeys.

[b]Why I’m still working on it[/b]

I cannot emphasise enough how well this game has held up.

We are playing it NOW, laughing out loud at how we are fkking up levels and solutions, then cheering when something finally works. The Hot Seat features came directly out of those evenings. It is pure joy to play still and I am sad this IP was left on the vine to die (we speak not of the current gacha mobile games).

For me, this is as much fun in 2026 as it was in 1992. Lemmings is a ridiculously good game, and I want more people to have that experience again.
