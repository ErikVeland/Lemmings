[b]Oh My! All Lemmings![/b]
[i]A proposed learning path through Classic Lemmings, from Fun to Expert[/i]

Lemmings is funny, inventive and rewarding. It can also stop a new player cold when the next level demands a trick they have never learned. The aim of this project is to make the journey enjoyable: learn a skill, practise it, combine it with something familiar, and gradually take on harder puzzles.

[b]Oh My! All Lemmings![/b] brings the Classic library into one shared progression. It includes [b]572 levels: 352 official levels and 220 selected fan-library levels from 85 packs[/b]. All 100 Oh No! More Lemmings levels are included. The official selection also covers the original Lemmings, Christmas and Holiday releases, and the port-exclusive and versus content in the Classic library.

The order mixes sources throughout. A level does not come early just because its original rank was Fun or Tame. A suitable fan level can appear anywhere its demands and teaching value fit.

[b]The learning curve[/b]
[list]
[*][b]Fun — levels 1–45[/b]
Single skills, repeated practice and simple combinations.
[*][b]Intermediate — levels 46–161[/b]
Combine familiar skills and learn to manage the crowd.
[*][b]Difficult — levels 162–445[/b]
Follow longer plans and work with tighter resources.
[*][b]Expert — levels 446–572[/b]
Bring precision, complex plans and coordination together.
[/list]

These are stages in this new path. They are not the original games' rank labels.

[b]How the order works[/b]

A low average difficulty score can hide one very demanding part of a puzzle. This order therefore considers the hardest observed demand as well as the overall estimate. The evidence covers techniques, solution length and complexity, timing, simultaneous workers, resource constraints and a limited estimate of deduction demands.

Levels sit in narrow difficulty bands. Within a band, the order favours skill preparation, spaces repeated practice and allows small relief steps. A combination waits for its easiest available isolated skill lessons. Two-skill combinations seek one earlier practice opportunity for each basic skill. Larger combinations seek two.

The current sequence passes that basic-skill preparation check. This means the opportunities appear in the playlist. It does not prove that a player has mastered each skill.

For example, [b]A task for blockers and bombers[/b] now follows an isolated blocker lesson. [b]Head for the Hills![/b], whose average score hid a high timing demand, moves from position 233 in the previous order to position 542, in Expert.

The stage boundaries and ordering weights are editorial estimates. They are not difficulty ratings measured from novice players. The original evidence scores remain unchanged. Small changes in overall difficulty are deliberate, and the hardest individual transitions remain open to review.

[b]Why these fan levels?[/b]

The audited Classic catalogue contains 6,374 entries. The 220 selected fan-library levels each have a winning replay validated against the native simulation. Their measured profiles are tied to those exact replays.

Selection excludes hands-free fan records and duplicates detected by gameplay signatures. The fan count comes from the available evidence and selection checks, rather than a fixed quota. Some fan-library packs contain ports or remakes, so “fan” below identifies the catalogue source rather than claiming every puzzle is an original fan design.

A successful replay proves that a route works. It does not prove that a new player will see that route. Likewise, failure to find a replay for another level does not prove that level is impossible.

[b]What still needs playtesting?[/b]

This is a revised candidate for a smoother learning curve, not a certified wall-free campaign. Two transitions still have flagged increases in timing demand:
[list]
[*][b]37. Thunder-Lemmings are go![/b] — Fun.
[*][b]542. Head for the Hills![/b] — Expert.
[/list]

These need novice playtesting for timing forgiveness and frustration. The rest of the path also needs player feedback on whether solutions feel readable and whether practice arrives at the right time. Only two automated flags does not mean only two possible human difficulty spikes.

The implementation has passed 94 automated tests. The rebuilt app loads this order and shows the current stage. All selected fan replays have passed native replay validation. These checks establish software behaviour and replay consistency, not the quality of a new player's experience.

In the app, [b]Try later[/b] parks a level without awarding a win. Parked levels remain available to revisit. This gives players room to keep exploring, but it is not a substitute for filling a difficulty gap.
